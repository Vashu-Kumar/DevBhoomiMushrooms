About Our Mushrooms

Everything you'd want to know about oyster mushrooms — what they are, how they grow, and why they're worth cooking with.

What are oyster mushrooms?

Oyster mushrooms are a variety of edible fungi named for their broad, fan- or oyster-shell-shaped caps. They typically grow in overlapping clusters, with colors ranging from pale grey and white to soft brown, depending on the variety.

Their texture is tender with a mild, slightly savory flavor, which makes them easy to pair with a wide range of cuisines. Unlike many mushrooms, oyster mushrooms grow naturally on decaying wood and organic matter, which is why they're often cultivated on straw or sawdust-based substrates.

They've become popular with home cooks and chefs alike for their versatility, quick cooking time, and ability to absorb the flavors of whatever they're cooked with.

Appearance Fan-shaped caps, grey to brown
Taste Mild, savory, slightly nutty
Growth On straw, sawdust or agricultural waste
Popularity Common in home and restaurant kitchens
